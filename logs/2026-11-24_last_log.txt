Abstract  Optimized floating-point inference is not reproducible across environments: the same model on different hardware, compilers or thread counts can produce different bits. We present tritify, an open-source runtime that executes the forward pass of real models -- decoder-only language models, Mixture-of-Experts decoders, and the Segment Anything 2.1 image/video stack -- with the entire run-time path in exact integer arithmetic; floating point is confined to offline quantization and the float reference used for verification. We make three separate claims and verify each. (1) Cross-hardware reproducibility: the integer path produces bit-identical output run to run, across CPU instruction-set architectures, and across CPU/GPU, carried by a reproducing SHA-256 of the output. (2) Decision fidelity: the integer path reproduces the float reference's decision on the models and inputs tested, with the numeric exceptions measured; per-layer fidelity through sixteen layers of OLMoE-1B-7B and of a 106-billion-parameter MoE shows no fidelity cliff. GLM-4.5-Air (106,852,251,264 parameters, 427 GB of integer codes) generated its answer across three machines, none of which can hold its integer codes in RAM, bit-identically to a single-host reference. The consequence is verifiable distributed inference: an untrusted server or a fleet of devices runs the model while an open scalar oracle re-derives the exact bytes; a guarantee against a dishonest server is future work. Abstract  Asking a vision-language model (VLM) to think step by step often makes it worse at spatial questions, even though the image stays in context. We show that short chain-of-thought (CoT) turns a perception problem into a transcription problem. Studying 15 open VLMs (2B-38B) and six frontier closed models, we rule out token count, turn format, and visual forgetting in open models and trace the failure through three stages. Abstract  Graphical user interface (GUI) agents are widely trained in executable GUI environments. Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Robot policies must adapt to the speed of moving objects, yet existing benchmarks rarely measure this ability across diverse tasks and speeds.
