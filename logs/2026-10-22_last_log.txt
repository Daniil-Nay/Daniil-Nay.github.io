Abstract VX-One is a small multi-task neural network that outputs calibrated typed decision primitives for perimeter triage, evaluated in a two-phase cascade against signature engines and baseline classifiers across multiple intrusion detection benchmarks.  Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small Perimeter triage faces a latency/semantics trade-off: signature engines are fast and format-safe but blind to unseen attacks, while generative models are semantic but slow and produce unparseable output. In late 2026 a class of commercial System One decision models (TypeSafe Jev; Microsoft-Decision-1) began selling typed decision primitives - binary gates, closed-set choices, ordinal scores - as product APIs, with no academic treatment. We present VX-One: a small multi-head network (100K-10M parameters) whose outputs are these primitives - a calibrated gate, a choice over a closed candidate set that always includes an explicit unknown option, and an ordinal score, trained multi-task, temperature-calibrated per head (~0.7-1.7 ms p50 on CPU). We evaluate it on UNSW-NB15, CIC-IDS2017, EMBER2024 against calibrated XGBoost, Random-Forest, MLP, Kitsune, and measure a two-phase cascade (Suricata with 52,674 Emerging-Threats rules -> VX-One) over five days of CIC-IDS2017. Findings: (i) VX-One wins detection (AUROC/FPR@95%TPR) on UNSW-NB15 and under temporal drift on CIC-IDS2017 (0.711 vs 0.637), but XGBoost wins multi-class attribution on all three benchmarks; (ii) leave-one-family-out gate detects never-seen families on 9/9 UNSW folds yet drops below chance on CIC recon family, and abstention threshold transfers poorly across benchmarks; (iii) on payload-less reconstructions signature engines resolve ~0.1% of flows - gray zone ~99.9% of traffic, recovered by Phase 2 (combined recall >=0.978); (iv) calibration collapses under temporal drift (ECE 0.04->0.40) for every method while ranking partially survives. We provide a measured account of where typed calibrated decisions help perimeter triage, and where they fail.

Abstract Most proposed reinforcement learning training methods for language models are not adopted by open-source frameworks, with adoption rates remaining low despite growing publication volumes.  Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small Practitioners use reinforcement learning methods for language models through training frameworks, so a method that a framework includes is one configuration change away for its users. We measure how often this happens. We trace seven open-source frameworks for RL post-training through their commit histories and link them to 6,939 screened arXiv papers from January 2025 to June 2026. Of 1,598 papers that propose a general RL training method, 37 reached at least one framework. Across five quarterly cohorts of papers, the number included within 180 days stayed between 5 and 7 while the cohorts grew from 72 to 386 papers. Compared with papers from the same quarters, included papers more often trained policies of 30B parameters or more, at 41 against 10 percent, and more often changed a coefficient, clip, mask or weight in the existing loss, at 29 against 9 percent. Both gaps hold without papers from the companies that maintain the frameworks, while code release was equally common in both groups. The frameworks' issues and pull requests show that few methods are turned away in public. More than nine in ten of the general methods no framework included have no thread there that cites or names them, and many proposals that do appear stay open without a reply. Two further records, the frameworks' issues and the technical reports of 13 large open-weight models, show that users rarely report training failures in public and that the reports state 8 of the 13 widely included methods. We release the data as a tracker that links training failures to framework implementations and lists each framework's contribution rules and earlier proposals.

Abstract WorldStream is an open-ended 3D world model that continuously expands terrain, processes language commands, and preserves edits through hierarchical planning and streaming compilation.  Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small We present WorldStream, an open-ended 3D world model with three simultaneous capabilities: it extends playable terrain beyond any pre-specified map, acts on open-ended language in the running world, and restores visited places with their edits. A hierarchical planner over a typed decision model emits structured decision state: Macro fixes settlement, ecology, transport, and hydrological structure across a group of tiles; Micro specializes that contract into tile semantics under measured terrain and accepted neighbor commitments. Deterministic local code compiles accepted plans into terrain, roads, settlements, vegetation, and entities. Streaming separates their reach: plans run ahead along the predicted corridor, compilation only where movement will soon need it, geometry behind the user released. One world record retains plans, seeds, object identities and mutable state, boundary commitments, and edits by attribute, revised as the user advances and edits; returning tiles recompile from it. The same record grounds interaction: the model acts on existing entities, including stateful NPCs that update as they act, edits the present region, or attaches intent to unplanned regions. Together they yield a world expanding under bounded budgets, connected across independently planned tiles, and interactive.
