Abstract Causal World Action Models enforce sparse routing to isolate action-relevant latent variables, ensuring identifiable causal structure without requiring temporal independence assumptions.  Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small World action models (WAMs) learn latent variables from observations and use these to both predict the evolution of the scene and to generate actions. The prediction has to be of the whole scene, whereas only a portion of the scene causes actions. WAMs fitted to demonstrations do not, in general, guarantee that they will generate actions solely from the latent causes of the demonstrated actions (the action-relevant latent variables), as the model could just as well learn to imitate in this way whilst entangling the action-relevant latent variables with the nuisance variables. We therefore separate out what causes action from what prediction depends on. We prove that under a sparsity constraint the latent variables used to generate actions are an invertible function of the action-relevant latent variables alone and the true structure is identified up to a permutation of the latent variables. This requires neither sufficient temporal dependence nor any assumptions on how the unobserved regimes evolve and holds even when the action-relevant latent variables are statistically dependent on the rest. We therefore propose CWAM (Causal WAM), which incorporates the constraint into a pretrained WAM by adding a sparse routing path to its action head, with the world modeling objective remaining unchanged. Experiments on both synthetic data and real-world robots validate the theory as well as showcase the improved efficiency in closed-loop success.

Abstract SciGame evaluates scientific discovery agents using structured cards with counterfactual environments to separate prior knowledge from empirical evidence, revealing that exact symbolic law recovery remains a key challenge.  Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small Large language model agents are increasingly used for scientific discovery. However, existing benchmarks mix law inference with programming, score data fitting instead of prediction on unseen inputs, and do not separate empirical evidence from pretrained priors. We introduce SciGame, a controlled benchmark that converts scientific literature into structured scientific cards. Each card yields a pair of Original and dimensionally consistent Counterfactual environments with the same task interface. SciGame contains a Historical collection of canonical laws and a live Temporal collection curated from papers published after model knowledge cutoffs. It evaluates agents in candidate-guided Identification and open-ended Discovery settings. Each setting runs under Prior-only, Fixed, and Adaptive protocols. Experiments on five frontier models yield three findings. First, familiar priors hurt performance once the governing relation is altered. Second, adaptive experimentation helps the strongest models but harms weaker ones under limited budgets. Third, good explanations can hide quantitative failures. Recovering the exact symbolic form remains the main bottleneck, even when agents interpret evidence correctly. Current agents can investigate systems actively, but inducing exact laws in unfamiliar settings remains an open challenge.

Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small Edge medical segmentation must reconcile small model size, high accuracy, and fast inference. Split-channel large-kernel blocks preserve spatial context with limited computation, while branch fusion removes redundant inference paths. Ternary Progressive Quantization-aware Training (TPQ) then adapts the fused network from deep to shallow layers, through the decoder, and finally to the input and prediction layers. These results demonstrate a path from accurate lightweight medical segmentation to compact low-bit hardware.

Abstract Training acceleration in diffusion transformers is driven by reorganization of AdaLN-Zero residual gates, and a simple radial objective promoting broader gate participation improves generation quality without auxiliary teachers.  Generated by thinkingmachines/Inkling-Small Training acceleration methods for diffusion transformers, such as REPA and dispersive regularization, are commonly explained by additional supervision, spatial-structure priors, or improved feature geometry. We revisit this view and uncover a striking internal change: the AdaLN-Zero residual gate α, which directly controls how conditioned updates enter the residual stream, is substantially reorganized by these methods. This observation motivates our hypothesis that acceleration arises, at least in part, from reshaping the residual gating pathway rather than solely from the feature structures explicitly imposed by auxiliary objectives. Supporting this view, accelerated models are markedly more sensitive to perturbations of α, and their gate geometry reflects semantic relationships among classes. We further introduce the normalized participation ratio (nPR) to characterize the channel-wise organization of the gates and find that, across REPA teachers, projector architectures, and radial-objective targets, broader gate participation accompanies better generation quality. Guided by this insight, we propose a simple teacher-free radial objective that promotes broader gate participation through feature-norm regularization, without pairwise feature comparisons. On ImageNet 256times256 with SiT-B/2, our method improves FID over dispersive loss while increasing mean gate participation over timesteps and classes, further supporting the link between gate organization and training acceleration. Our results identify residual-gate reorganization as a measurable axis of diffusion training acceleration and suggest a simple direction for further acceleration.
