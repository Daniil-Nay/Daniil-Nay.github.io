Abstract  Frontier LLM agents that query databases with tools are among the most accurate text-to-SQL systems, but per-query calls to a hosted model are slow, costly, and expose sensitive organizational queries to external APIs. For example, a Claude Opus 5.5 agent serving 10,000 queries a day costs over $70,000 a year at list price. To what extent can local hardware already owned by an organization offload this burden without compromising the agent's accuracy? We present LuthorSQL, a non-autoregressive 7B text-to-SQL model that serves as a rapid System 1 gate for a slower LLM agent (System 2), escalating queries only when uncertain. Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Streaming video understanding aims to continuously process incoming visual observations, making the retention and retrieval of relevant information increasingly important as the video history grows. Although existing benchmarks include memory-related questions, question-answering accuracy alone does not reliably measure memory capabilities. Incorrect predictions may arise from failures in visual understanding rather than forgetting, whereas correct predictions may rely on linguistic priors or recent observations without requiring historical memory. To address these ambiguities, we introduce MemInStream, a benchmark dedicated to evaluating memory capabilities in streaming video understanding. Overall, MemInStream provides a comprehensive and reliable benchmark for systematically evaluating memory capabilities in streaming video understanding.

Abstract  Deployment objectives often specify desired outcomes without providing revised trajectories, while parts of the original reference must still be followed. We present OneSteer, a one-step diffusion whole-body controller that combines general motion tracking with selective test-time steering. To accommodate new objectives, differentiable task costs guide optimization of the generator's input noise with frozen weights, while body-group reference masks relax selected tracking requirements. Predicted state-action plans provide a shared interface for task optimization and feedback-conditioned execution. For nominal tracking, the model trained from scratch achieves the lowest local tracking error among the evaluated baselines on successful held-out AMASS and BONES clips. For steering, one frozen distilled controller adapts across diverse deployment objectives in simulation, raising the clean passage rate under a 1.25 m bar from 2% to 90%. Abstract Distilled few-step diffusion models exhibit diversity collapse because activation variance is concentrated into fewer directions in an intermediate block at the initial denoising step, and ReDiverse restores sample diversity by reintroducing teacher variance directions through adapter fine-tuning or training-free activation steering while preserving generation quality.  Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Distilling diffusion models into few-step student models substantially reduces sampling latency, but induces severe diversity collapse: samples generated from different random seeds show minimal variation. In this work, we investigate the root cause of this failure by probing the internal representations of student models relative to their teachers. We find that distilled students maintain total activation variance comparable to their teachers, but concentrate it into substantially fewer directions. This directional collapse is localized to an identifiable intermediate block at the initial denoising step. Consequently, diversity collapse stems from a misallocation of activation variance rather than an inherent loss of generative capacity. Guided by this finding, we propose ReDiverse to reinstate the missing variance directions. At training time, ReDiverse-FT fine-tunes a lightweight adapter that regresses student activations onto the teacher's, reviving the teacher's feature variance characteristic. At inference time, ReDiverse-TF introduces a training-free mechanism that steers activations along precomputed teacher principal directions. Across image and video distillation benchmarks, ReDiverse consistently restores sample diversity while preserving generation quality, further supported by the user study.

Abstract  Robots handle much more than rigid objects: towels, cables, water, sand. The simulators used to train robots at scale are strongest on rigid bodies, and adding another material usually means a second engine and hand-written glue between the two. We present Alloy, an open-source physics engine for robot learning in which rigid robots, cloth, cables, liquids, granular material and soft bodies share one scene. Each object is described once; Alloy assigns it to a solver suited to its material, couples the solvers inside every control step, and runs the scene in many parallel worlds on one GPU, or on a CPU. Every world carries a health grade, to help tell a failure of the physics from a failure of the policy. Alloy is built on Newton, MuJoCo Warp and Warp, and it is written to be driven by coding agents: its documentation ships inside the package, every error names its fix, and the engine is exposed as tools over the Model Context Protocol. In a cold test, agents that had never seen Alloy installed a pre-release from the package index and trained policies for walking, cable handling and pouring on a single GPU. We also report how throughput grows with the number of worlds, depth-camera throughput, agreement with closed-form references, and a reliability test over a thousand worlds.
